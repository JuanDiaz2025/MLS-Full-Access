## The three photos

| When | Shot | Must show |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival, before 10:00 | Front of property, wide | Address or a recognizable elevation, everyone on site |
| Midday | Work in progress | The actual surface being worked |
| Close | What got covered / closed | End-of-day state |

Plus: **receipt photo same day, same job**, for anything bought that day.

Filename format is in `docs/decision-process.md`. Caption if the app will not
take a filename.

## The rule

**No arrival photo before 10:00 → the day's hours are questioned, not paid, until
the site lead explains it.**

This is not distrust. It is the only thing that protects the company and the
crew in an argument about a day nobody wrote down. A photo at 9:05 ends the
argument in five seconds.

4. Cherry carries a running weekly total on both and flags at 32 hours, not 40.
   A flag at 40 is already too late.

**Pay side — read this before you enforce it:**

Capping the *schedule* is legal and correct. Refusing to *pay* for hours
somebody actually worked is not, in California — hours worked get paid, and
past 8 in a day or 40 in a week they get paid at overtime rate, even if the
overtime was unauthorized. So the enforcement is on the front end: block the
schedule, block the clock-in, send them home. If unauthorized hours get worked
anyway, they get paid and then it is a discipline conversation, not a payroll
deduction. Run any version of this past your payroll provider or an employment
attorney before it goes to the crew in writing.

The goal here is real and reachable: stop paying overtime nobody approved. Do it
by controlling the schedule, not by short-paying a timesheet.
